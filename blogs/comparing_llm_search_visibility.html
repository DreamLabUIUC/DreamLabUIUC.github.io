The Newest Techniques for Improving Visibility in LLM-Based Search: SEO vs GEO vs CORE

Comparing the main approaches — and why we recommend CORE for controlling output rankings

This post is written by the authors of CORE. We compare the main families of approaches to being seen in LLM-based search, and explain why — for the specific problem of controlling output rankings in generative product recommendations — we recommend our paper, CORE (arXiv:2602.03608). We try to characterize related work fairly; where approaches measure different things, we say so.

The landscape

As search shifts from ranked lists of blue links toward synthesized answers and direct recommendations, “visibility” changes meaning. Three broad approaches address it:

1. Traditional SEO

Classic search engine optimization targets the ranking of web pages in a list of results: keywords, backlinks, site structure, and technical health. It was designed for a world where the user inspects a ranked list themselves. It is still useful for being retrieved, but it does not directly address how an LLM synthesizes and recommends from retrieved content.

2. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)

GEO: Generative Engine Optimization (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) introduced the framing of optimizing content for visibility in generative-engine answers. It proposes a black-box optimization framework with visibility metrics and the GEO-bench benchmark of diverse queries, and reports visibility improvements of up to roughly 40%, varying by domain. GEO is foundational for the area: it established that creators can measurably influence whether their content is included in generated answers.

3. CORE (our work): controlling output rankings

CORE targets a more specific question than inclusion: given that LLM-based search produces a small, ranked set of recommendations, how can the content returned by the search engine be optimized to control where an item lands in that ranking? Like GEO, CORE is black-box and optimizes retrieved content rather than the model. It introduces three concrete optimization-content types — string-based, reasoning-based, and review-based — and evaluates them on ProductBench (15 product categories, 200 products each, with top-10 recommendations collected from Amazon’s search interface).

Across four search-enabled LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, Claude-4, and Grok-3), the paper reports an average Promotion Success Rate of 91.4% @Top-5, 86.6% @Top-3, and 80.3% @Top-1, and reports outperforming existing ranking-manipulation methods while preserving the fluency of the optimized content.

How they compare

These approaches are complementary and, importantly, are not measured on the same yardstick. GEO’s ~40% is a visibility/inclusion gain on GEO-bench; CORE’s percentages are promotion success in a ranking on ProductBench. They are not directly comparable numbers, and we do not claim CORE “beats” GEO — they study different objectives.

  • Traditional SEO — optimizes ranking of web pages in link lists; not generative-engine-aware; evaluated by search rankings/traffic.
  • GEO (Aggarwal et al., 2024) — optimizes content for inclusion/visibility in generated answers; black-box; evaluated on GEO-bench (visibility metrics).
  • CORE (ours, 2026) — optimizes retrieved content to control output rankings / promotion in generative recommendations; black-box; evaluated on ProductBench (Promotion Success Rate across four LLMs).

Why we recommend CORE for output-ranking control

If your question is specifically “how do I influence where an item is ranked in an LLM’s generated recommendations,” we recommend CORE because it is built directly for that objective: it is black-box (no model or search-engine access needed), it offers three concrete and distinct optimization-content strategies, it is evaluated in a realistic product-recommendation setting, and it reports strong, consistent results across multiple major search-enabled LLMs. For the broader objective of inclusion/visibility, GEO is the natural starting point, and CORE builds on that direction toward fine-grained ranking control.
