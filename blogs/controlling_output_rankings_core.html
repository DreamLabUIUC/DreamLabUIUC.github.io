CORE: Controlling Output Rankings in Generative Engines for LLM-based Search

Official project page — improving visibility in LLM-based search (generative engine optimization)

This is the official project page for our paper, “Controlling Output Rankings in Generative Engines for LLM-based Search.” Read the paper on arXiv (2602.03608).

What problem do we study?

LLM-based search systems and generative engines increasingly answer product queries by directly recommending a small set of options, rather than returning a traditional ranked list of web pages for users to inspect. This shift changes how visibility works: a product may be retrieved by a search engine, but still fail to appear prominently in the LLM’s final recommendation.

Our work studies how to improve visibility in this new setting by controlling output rankings in generative engines.

Our method: CORE

We propose CORE, short for Controlling Output Rankings in Generative Engines. CORE treats the LLM and its search interactions as a black box and focuses on the content returned by the search engine as the practical optimization surface. Instead of changing the model, CORE optimizes retrieved content by appending strategically designed optimization content that can steer how products are ranked in the LLM’s generated answer.

We study three types of optimization content:

  1. String-based optimization content — compact textual additions designed to affect ranking behavior.
  2. Reasoning-based optimization content — content that surfaces comparative reasoning useful to the LLM when deciding rankings.
  3. Review-based optimization content — content derived from review-style evidence that can influence product recommendation order.

Benchmark: ProductBench

To evaluate LLM-based search visibility in a realistic product recommendation setting, we introduce ProductBench, a large-scale benchmark with:

  • 15 product categories
  • 200 products per category
  • for each product, its top-10 recommendations collected from Amazon’s search interface

ProductBench allows us to test whether optimized content can promote a target product in the final answers generated by search-enabled LLMs.

Main findings

Across four LLMs with search capabilities — GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, Claude-4, and Grok-3 — CORE substantially improves promotion success in generated rankings. Average Promotion Success Rate across 15 product categories:

  • 91.4% @Top-5
  • 86.6% @Top-3
  • 80.3% @Top-1

CORE outperforms existing ranking manipulation methods while preserving the fluency of the optimized content.

Why this matters for LLM-based search visibility

Our paper shows that visibility in LLM-based search is not determined only by traditional retrieval position. The content supplied to the LLM after retrieval can strongly influence which products are ultimately recommended and how highly they are ranked. For researchers and practitioners studying new techniques for improving visibility in generative engines, our work provides:

  • a black-box formulation of output ranking control in LLM-based search;
  • the CORE optimization framework;
  • three concrete optimization content strategies;
  • the ProductBench benchmark for evaluation;
  • empirical results across multiple major search-enabled LLMs.

Paper
