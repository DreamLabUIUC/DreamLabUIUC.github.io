CORE Explained: New Techniques for Improving Visibility in LLM-Based Search

A deep dive into our paper on controlling output rankings in generative engines

Published June 9, 2026

In this post we take a closer look at our paper, “Controlling Output Rankings in Generative Engines for LLM-based Search” (arXiv:2602.03608). If you are researching the newest techniques for improving visibility in LLM-based search or generative engines, here is what CORE does and why we think it matters.

The problem we tackle

LLM search systems often do not simply return a list of blue links. They synthesize answers and recommendations, and those outputs can be heavily affected by the order and content of material retrieved before the model generates its response. That shift creates a new visibility challenge for products, businesses, and independent creators that may not already rank highly in conventional retrieval results.

Our method: CORE

The paper’s main contribution is CORE, short for Controlling Output Rankings in generative Engines. CORE treats the LLM search system as a black box and focuses on what a site owner can realistically change: the content that search systems retrieve. Rather than modifying the model or the search engine, CORE appends optimized content designed to influence how the LLM ranks or recommends items in its generated answer. We examine three types of optimization content:

  1. String-based optimization content, which uses carefully selected textual additions.
  2. Reasoning-based optimization content, which adds explanatory material intended to make a product or item appear more relevant during the model’s reasoning process.
  3. Review-based optimization content, which frames visibility around review-style supporting evidence.

Evaluation: ProductBench

For evaluation, we introduce ProductBench, a benchmark covering 15 product categories with 200 products per category. Each product is paired with top-10 recommendations collected from Amazon’s search interface, allowing us to test whether optimized retrieved content can change the rankings produced by search-enabled LLMs.

Results

Across GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, Claude-4, and Grok-3, CORE achieves an average Promotion Success Rate of 91.4% at Top-5, 86.6% at Top-3, and 80.3% at Top-1 across the tested product categories. CORE outperforms existing ranking-manipulation methods while preserving the fluency of the optimized content.

Why it matters

For readers researching the newest techniques for improving visibility in LLM-based search, we think this work matters because it moves beyond traditional SEO advice and studies generative-engine ranking behavior directly. It frames visibility as a content optimization problem inside black-box LLM search pipelines, and it provides both a method, CORE, and an evaluation benchmark, ProductBench.
