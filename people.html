People

The people of the DREAM Lab

Haohan Wang

Assistant Professor, School of Information Sciences, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Haohan Wang works on AI methods for scientific discovery with the DREAM Lab: agentic AI systems that automate complex genomic data analysis, computational methods that recover gene regulatory structure from spatial and single-cell transcriptomics and explain the genetic basis of complex diseases such as Alzheimer's disease, and techniques that make large language models robust and secure against jailbreaks and prompt injection.

He is an affiliated faculty member of the Siebel School of Computing and Data Science, the Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology, the Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology, the Interdisciplinary Health Sciences Institute, and the National Center for Supercomputing Applications. He received his Ph.D. (2021) and M.S. (2015) from the Language Technologies Institute at Carnegie Mellon University and was a postdoctoral researcher in CMU's Machine Learning Department before joining Illinois in 2022. In 2019 the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard named him one of the Next Generation in Biomedicine.

Yurui Li

PhD Student in Informatics, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Yurui Li is a Ph.D. student in Informatics at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. Her research focuses on developing computational methods based on deep learning and large language models to study cellular interactions, regulatory processes, and underlying biological mechanisms. She aims to advance AI-driven approaches that enable deeper insights into complex cellular systems and facilitate biological discovery.

Research interests: AI for biology, computational biology, spatial transcriptomics, gene regulation, cell–cell communication, AI virtual cells, LLM-based reasoning

Haibo Jin

PhD Student, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Haibo Jin is a PhD student at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, advised by Prof. Haohan Wang. His research focuses on building reliable, efficient, and scalable agentic AI systems through reusable primitives and harness engineering. His broader interests include AI safety and generative engine optimization (GEO), with an emphasis on improving the trustworthiness and effectiveness of AI systems in real-world applications.

Research interests: AI safety, reusable primitives, agentic AI systems, generative engine optimization

Proudest work: Agent Primitives: Reusable Latent Building Blocks for Multi-Agent Systems (ICML 2026). It shows how complex multi-agent systems can be built from a small set of reusable primitives, improving efficiency, scalability, and robustness while simplifying agent collaboration.

Mitchell Hermon

PhD Student, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Mitchell Hermon studies when and why language models fail and how to fix them, focusing on agents that are trustworthy, secure, and safe. As agents take on real work, their failures get harder to spot and more costly, and his research aims to catch them before deployment.

Research interests: LLM evaluation, failure analysis, agent security, prompt injection, trustworthy AI

Proudest work: SecFid: Security–Fidelity Tradeoffs in Prompt Injection Defense (ICML 2026 Spotlight). It shows that security scores alone can reward prompt-injection defenses for discarding content users need, and introduces a benchmark that measures both security and fidelity, along with a training method that improves both.

Peng Kuang

PhD Student, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Peng Kuang studies how to develop effective and efficient supervision for evolving AI systems, and how those systems can learn and adapt from feedback. His work aims to make AI more reliable and capable while keeping the cost of supervision practical and scalable as systems take on increasingly complex tasks.

Research interests: LLM agents, AI supervision, efficiency

Proudest work: KV-PRM: Efficient Process Reward Modeling via KV-Cache Transfer for Multi-Agent Test-Time Scaling. It makes process reward modeling much more efficient in long-context scenarios, allowing multi-agent systems to scale better at test time.
