How Should an AI Theorem Prover Pick a Proof Method?

October 15, 2026

  • Read More

Most LLM provers pick a proof method by retrieving ones that look similar to the theorem, which misses many that would work. Letting each of 82 reusable methods propose how it would be used put a correct one in the top 5 for 95.0% of Putnam problems.

Does Recursive Self-Improvement Make AI Agents Forget?

October 14, 2026

  • Read More

When LLM agents rewrite their own workflows, skills, weights or memory to master new tasks, they often get worse at tasks they had already mastered. A simple retention constraint raised retained task success from 41.8% to 52.8% while the agent also learned the new tasks better.

Is AI Math Just Search? What LLMs Miss Between Math Fields

October 12, 2026

  • Read More

Search plus a checker can certify a proof, but not whether a model tracks what a theorem assumes. When seven LLMs restated theorems in a more general field of mathematics, 60.6% of rewrites silently widened the claim and none narrowed it, an error proof checkers do not see.

Vision Transformer Robustness: The Inputs a ViT Cannot See

October 9, 2026

  • Read More

Many vision transformers are exactly blind to most directions of change in each image patch: a ViT-S/32 cannot see 2,688 of the 3,072 input dimensions per patch. Our CPAL 2025 paper turns that nullspace into a fine-tuning method that raised ViT-S's average robustness score from 26.5 to 34.5.

Can Aligned LLMs Jailbreak Themselves? Inside the SLIP Attack

October 7, 2026

  • Read More

Our COLM 2026 paper shows that a safety-aligned LLM can supply every step of its own jailbreak. SLIP needs no attacker model and reached 94.7% average attack success across 11 LLMs in about 7.9 queries, while per-turn defenses left it at 57–83%.

The Hidden Cost of Prompt Injection Defense

July 11, 2026

  • Read More

Prompt injection defenses are graded on attack success rate, which cannot tell a model that faithfully processes injected text as data from one that simply deletes it. Our SecFid benchmark makes that difference measurable — and across 48 models and defenses, none achieves both security and fidelity. The most secure systems drop between a quarter and a half of the content they should keep.
